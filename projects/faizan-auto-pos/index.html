Faizan Auto — POS System

A point-of-sale and inventory system for an auto parts business, built on the MERN stack.

Role
Frontend Developer (MERN)
Organisation
MindGigspk
Category
Full Stack
Timeline
Sep 2023 — Sep 2024

Overview

A point-of-sale and inventory system for an auto parts retailer, covering the full trading cycle: buying stock from suppliers, selling it over the counter, handling returns in both directions, and reporting what the shop actually made.

An auto parts catalogue is the awkward part. A shop carries thousands of items that are only meaningful in relation to a vehicle — the same part number fits one model and not another — so the catalogue is keyed on model, brand, item code and unit rather than on a flat product name. By the time this screenshot was taken the system held 2,169 stock items.

My Role

I built the frontend as a junior MERN developer at MindGigspk, working under senior mentorship: the screens, the Redux Toolkit state and RTK Query data layer, form validation across the sale, purchase and product flows, and the report views. This was one of four full-stack applications I worked on there, and the one with the most modules.

Key Features

What the system actually does.

Sales and purchases
Multi-line sale and purchase entry with live stock quantity, per-line pricing, and partial payment tracked as paid versus remaining.
Returns in both directions
Sale returns and purchase returns as first-class flows, so stock and money both move back correctly instead of being corrected by hand.
Model-keyed catalogue
Parts are filed by vehicle model, brand, item code and unit — the way the counter staff actually look them up.
Low-stock alerts
Each item carries its own alert quantity, so the reorder threshold reflects how fast that specific part moves.
Supplier and customer ledgers
Both sides of the counter tracked, with outstanding balances carried against the right party.
Sale, purchase and P&L reports
Separate reporting for sales, purchases, expenses and profit and loss, rather than one dashboard the owner has to interpret.
Owner-editable settings
Vehicle models, payment types and expense categories are managed in the UI, so the shop does not need a developer to add a category.

What it's built with.

Challenges & Solutions

The problems that shaped the build, and what I did about them.

A parts catalogue is not a product list

Thousands of items whose identity depends on the vehicle they fit. A flat name field makes the catalogue unsearchable the moment two brands make the same part.

Solution: Modelled the vehicle model as its own managed entity that products reference, rather than as free text on the product. The owner maintains the model list in settings, and every part is filed against it.

Stock has to stay true across four different flows

A sale decrements stock, a purchase increments it, and both returns reverse their own direction. Any one of them getting it wrong silently corrupts the inventory count that the whole shop runs on.

Solution: Kept every stock movement tied to the document that caused it — sale, purchase, sale return, purchase return — so the current quantity is derived from a traceable history instead of being edited in place.

Counter staff are not data-entry clerks

A sale gets recorded while a customer waits. A form that demands every field in a fixed order loses to a paper notebook.

Solution: Made the sale screen add line items without leaving the page, default what could be defaulted, and show stock quantity inline so the seller never has to check availability in another screen.

Results

What shipped, and what changed because of it.

  • A catalogue of 2,000+ parts managed in one system, filed by the vehicle model they fit.
  • Sales, purchases and both return directions all move stock through the same traceable path.
  • The owner reads sales, purchases, expenses and profit and loss as separate reports instead of reconciling them by hand.
  • Models, payment types and expense categories are editable in the UI — no developer needed to add one.
